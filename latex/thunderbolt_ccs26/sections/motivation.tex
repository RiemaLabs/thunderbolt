\section{Design Rationale}\label{sec:motivation}

This section motivates the combination of the two primitives recalled in \autoref{sec:background}---verifiable encryption of discrete logarithms (VEDL) and verifiable encrypted secret sharing (VESS)---and explains why their composition is both necessary and sufficient for asynchronous off-chain UTXO transfer under a fixed on-chain key.

\paragraph{The binding requirement.}
A correct transfer step must produce three cryptographic objects simultaneously: a Paillier ciphertext of $x_{s+1} = x_s + r_s$ under Bob's key, a Paillier ciphertext of the refresh $r_s$ under the third party's key, and a public anchor delta $D_s = r_s\,H$ on the elliptic curve. All three objects must be \emph{bound to the same integer}; otherwise a dishonest party can publish inconsistent values and break the fixed-key invariant $(x_s + y_s)\,G = P$.

\paragraph{VEDL for per-step correctness.}
VEDL (\autoref{sec:bg-vedl}) provides exactly this binding. Its shared Sigma response $z$ forces the Paillier plaintext and the elliptic-curve discrete log to be the same integer, without revealing it. We instantiate VEDL in two ways: the Transfer Consistency Proof uses a single-receiver instance to bind the holder secret $x_s$ under $X_s$ to its Paillier ciphertext, while the Invoice Consistency Proof extends VEDL to a multi-receiver setting---a single shared $z$ simultaneously binds one refresh value $r_s$ to the anchor delta $D_s$ and to two Paillier ciphertexts under different keys.

\paragraph{VESS for threshold trust degradation.}
VEDL alone, however, concentrates the third-party role in a single actor whose compromise exposes the full lock key. VESS (\autoref{sec:bg-vess}) removes this single point of failure: the refresh $r_s$ is verifiably encrypted as a $(t,n)$-Shamir sharing, so that any $t$ honest members can reconstruct $r_s$ and perform $y_{s+1} = y_s - r_s$, while no coalition of fewer than $t$ learns anything. This substitution is modular: VESS replaces the single-party ciphertext $\mathrm{ct}_C$ with a set of verifiably encrypted shares, and neither the Invoice nor the Transfer Consistency Proof changes form.

\paragraph{Necessity of the composition.}
Neither primitive alone suffices. VEDL without VESS forces a single trusted third party, concentrating a point of failure that is unacceptable for a payment-channel deployment. VESS without VEDL distributes the third-party role but cannot bind the shared Shamir secret to the on-chain elliptic-curve anchor---an observer would have no way to verify that the anchor delta $D_s$ is consistent with the encrypted refresh shares. Together, VEDL supplies the \emph{per-step correctness guarantee} under a shared integer witness, and VESS supplies \emph{threshold trust degradation} for the third-party role.
