\begin{abstract}
We present \thunderbolt, an off-chain protocol that transfers Bitcoin UTXO ownership in \textbf{$\approx$1\,s}, requires \textbf{no channel graph, no routing, and no liquidity rebalancing}, and lets the \textbf{recipient be offline} at the time of transfer. A single UTXO is locked once on-chain under a fixed public key; ownership then passes through an unbounded sequence of holders; each transfer is a purely off-chain, asynchronous operation whose on-chain cost is zero. The chain sees exactly two transactions regardless of how many transfers occur.

The cryptographic core is a pair of Sigma-protocol NIZK proofs, the \emph{Invoice Consistency Proof} and the \emph{Transfer Consistency Proof}, which jointly enforce a ``one add, one subtract'' invariant on an additively shared key. Both instantiate Camenisch--Shoup verifiable encryption of discrete logarithms without trusted setup or circuit-level tooling. When the committee is a threshold group, Simple VESS (Verifiable Encrypted Secret Sharing) distributes the committee share under a standard $(t,n)$-honesty assumption, the only trust requirement \thunderbolt introduces beyond the base chain; routing and liquidity constraints are absent by construction.

On a commodity laptop, a complete off-chain transfer finishes in \textbf{1.05\,s} with a combined proof size of \textbf{3.8\,KB}. Against SNARK backends on the same relations, our proofs are \textbf{100--1000$\times$ faster}, as large-modulus Paillier arithmetic over $\mathbb{Z}_{N^2}$ requires a disproportionately large number of constraints in circuit-based proving systems.
\end{abstract}
